    How it's measured
    • On start the monitor checks whether UDP reaches the server. If it does, the whole session pings over UDP, like game traffic: a 4-byte probe through a WebRTC data channel with retransmissions turned off, so a lost packet stays lost. If not (a firewall, or the server isn't reachable over UDP), the session pings with HTTP requests over TCP. The status card shows which one is used; the event log says why UDP wasn't.
    • Ping is the round trip of one probe. Min, max, average and percentiles cover the whole session.
    • Jitter is the mean absolute difference between consecutive pings over the last 10 seconds. Min and max are the calmest and the worst 10 seconds of the session; max jump is the largest single change.
    • Lost: no reply within 2 seconds. Over UDP this is real packet loss. TCP resends lost packets, so over TCP small loss mostly shows up as spikes instead.
    • Spike: a reply more than twice the median and at least 50 ms above it.
    • Drop: lost pings in a row (at least two) covering at least a second. Downtime is the total length of drops, uptime is the share of the monitored time without them.
    • The probe's clock runs in a background worker, so it keeps its pace while the tab is hidden. If the computer sleeps, that time is marked as paused and not counted. Browsers with a memory or energy saver may unload idle tabs after a while: add this site to its exceptions for very long sessions.
